Heirs of the Prophet’s uncle al-Abbas, they overthrew the Umayyads, built Baghdad and presided over the golden age of Islamic learning.     Family tree

    The Abbasids, the Prophet ﷺ and the line of Ali were all Banu Hashim, a clan of Quraysh descended from Hashim, son of Abd Manaf. The Abbasids descend from al-Abbas, the Prophet’s uncle; the Alids from Ali and Fatima, the Prophet’s daughter. That shared descent is why the revolution could call for ‘a chosen one from the family of Muhammad’, and why Alid rebels later used the same claim against the Abbasids.

      The Baghdad line, caliph to caliph

      Each card says how the caliph was related to the one before. After 861 the succession was often decided by soldiers or protectors, which is why brothers, cousins and uncles follow one another.

      Through Ibn Khaldun’s lens

      In the Muqaddimah, Ibn Khaldun (1332–1406) argued that dynasties rise on ʿasabiyya, solidarity forged in hardship, and that luxury wears it away over about four 40-year generations. The Umayyads ran the cycle in 89 years. Read the same way, the first Abbasid cycle ran about 110 years; after 861 others held the solidarity while the caliph kept the legitimacy. This mapping is an interpretation, not Ibn Khaldun’s own list.
